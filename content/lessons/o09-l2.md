# Paths and cardinality

## What you will be able to do

Build fixtures with zero, one, and two different employee identifiers.

## The problem

You are working with workforce and project data that will be used by another team. Your result must have a clear meaning, survive incomplete records, and be reproducible. This lesson focuses on **paths and cardinality** within shacl foundations.

## Build the mental model

A property shape follows sh:path from each focus node, then applies minCount and maxCount to its value nodes. Exactly one requires both constraints. RDF graphs collapse duplicate identical triples before cardinality is checked.

## Worked example

Read the example before running it. Identify the input facts or columns, the relationship being expressed, and the expected result.

```turtle
@prefix ex: <https://example.org/> .
@prefix rdf: <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
@prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
@prefix owl: <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
@prefix xsd: <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .
@prefix sh: <http://www.w3.org/ns/shacl#> .
ex:PersonShape sh:property [ sh:path ex:employeeId ;
 sh:minCount 1 ; sh:maxCount 1 ] .
```

## A mistake worth recognizing

> maxCount 1 alone permits zero values.

Construct a small counterexample that would expose this mistake. Prefer three carefully chosen records to a large dataset whose behavior is hard to explain.
