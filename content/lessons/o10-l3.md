# Closed shapes and extension

## What you will be able to do

Allow rdf:type and employeeId while rejecting an accidental misspelled property.

## The problem

You are working with workforce and project data that will be used by another team. Your result must have a clear meaning, survive incomplete records, and be reproducible. This lesson focuses on **closed shapes and extension** within advanced shacl.

## Build the mental model

A closed shape limits allowed outgoing properties, subject to explicitly ignored predicates. Use it where a narrow contract is intentional. Closing a general-purpose entity shape can make harmless vocabulary extensions fail validation.

## Worked example

Read the example before running it. Identify the input facts or columns, the relationship being expressed, and the expected result.

```turtle
@prefix ex: <https://example.org/> .
@prefix rdf: <http://www.w3.org/1999/02/22-rdf-syntax-ns#> .
@prefix rdfs: <http://www.w3.org/2000/01/rdf-schema#> .
@prefix owl: <http://www.w3.org/2002/07/owl#> .
@prefix xsd: <http://www.w3.org/2001/XMLSchema#> .
@prefix sh: <http://www.w3.org/ns/shacl#> .
ex:PersonShape sh:closed true ; sh:ignoredProperties ( rdf:type ) .
```

## A mistake worth recognizing

> Closed shapes do not automatically forbid every incoming relationship.

Construct a small counterexample that would expose this mistake. Prefer three carefully chosen records to a large dataset whose behavior is hard to explain.
