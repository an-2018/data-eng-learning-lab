## The problem

You are working with workforce and project data that will be used by another team. Your result must have a clear meaning, survive incomplete records, and be reproducible. This lesson focuses on **handle skewed joins** within join strategies, skew, caching, and adaptive execution.

## Build the mental model

A hot key can make one join task dominate the job. Isolate the skew pattern, then consider adaptive skew handling, selective salting, pre-aggregation, or a different data layout.

## Worked example

## A mistake worth recognizing

> Salting every key increases data volume and complexity even when only one key is skewed.

Construct a small counterexample that would expose this mistake. Prefer three carefully chosen records to a large dataset whose behavior is hard to explain.
